# SoundEtch

Web audio recorder and editor

## Timeline display modes

Use each track's three-dots menu to switch between its waveform and a WebGPU-rendered spectrogram. The selected mode is session-only and resets to the waveform when a project is opened or the page is reloaded. If WebGPU is unavailable, the spectrogram view keeps its editing overlays on the selected color scheme's minimum-dB background; switching back to the waveform remains available.

Open **Settings** from the application menu to configure spectrogram rendering and analysis. Settings are grouped into collapsed, expandable sections. Color schemes include Viridis (default), Magma, Inferno, and Grayscale. FFT size (default 2,048 samples), analysis window (Blackman, Blackman-Harris, or Hann), hop size (512 samples), mel-frequency bands (128), the color scheme, and the minimum-level slider (default -96 dB) are saved in the browser and apply to all spectrogram tracks. Analysis changes clear the in-memory spectrogram tile cache; changing only the color scheme redraws cached tiles without recomputing them. Audio and waveform rendering are unchanged.

The **Downloaded models** settings section reports the persistent browser-cache footprint of DeepFilterNet, Granite, and Silero VAD separately. **Unload** removes that model's cached files, including stale Granite or VAD revision caches, without interrupting a model runtime already in use. The browser downloads those files again when a later runtime needs them.

## Transcription timeline overlays

Tracks with transcription show a dedicated lane below the waveform or spectrogram, keeping labels clear of the audio display. Every placement projects the clip's sparse, ordered source-sample ranges onto its timeline position, preserving alignment through moves, splits, trims, repeated placements, and display-mode changes. Range labels and their real start/end boundaries are clipped to the visible placement without filling untranscribed gaps or creating boundaries at trim and viewport edges.

Transcription ranges persist with the clip in IndexedDB and project archives. Clips created by older versions load with an empty transcription.

Click a transcript interval in the timeline lane to open the read-only **Transcript** panel and select that specific placed occurrence. When stopped or monitoring, this also selects its trim-clamped timeline interval. The panel lists every placed occurrence in timeline order, including repeated and offscreen placements; trims clamp each atom's interactive timing. Persisted multiword phrases remain one timed atom.

VAD-detected speech regions appear as separate paragraphs, including pauses whose inference padding overlaps. The 20-second inference chunks do not introduce paragraph breaks. Each word retains its source-region onset, preserving paragraph membership through trims, splits, repeated placements, persistence, and undo/redo. Previously generated transcripts without this metadata remain one paragraph until regenerated.

During playback, the panel highlights the current timed atom in green and scrolls newly active occurrences into view without changing the native text selection. Gaps clear the playback highlight, pausing retains it, and stopping clears it. Repeated and trimmed occurrences follow their placed timings; a persisted multiword phrase remains one timed atom.

Click a panel atom to reveal its occurrence and move the cursor to its start when stopped or monitoring. During playback, recording, or their paused states, clicks only reveal the occurrence without changing transport, cursor, or the timeline selection. Drag native text selection to select complete timed atoms on that track; keyboard text selection also updates the timeline range. While stopped or monitoring, panel drags enter pointer selection mode, and selecting a time range clears the position marker just like a timeline drag. Release, blur, closing the panel, or replacing its transcript data ends the panel gesture. Timeline selections appear in the panel as one continuous native text range from the first to the last intersecting atom, including intervening spaces, without snapping timeline boundaries to words. The panel follows committed edits and undo/redo, and closes when its track or project is removed.

To generate a transcript, select a region on one or more tracks, right-click the selection, and choose **Generate transcription**. Generation runs locally in the browser with the English-only IBM Granite Speech model and Silero VAD speech segmentation. Word boundaries come from transcript-conditioned CTC posterior medians refined by bounded Silero and energy evidence. The first run downloads about 313 MB: a 0.6 MB derived timing graph from the same origin, the pinned 312 MB model weights and support assets, and a 0.6 MB voice-activity model. Cache persistence is best-effort and never blocks model startup. Speech regions are detected with Silero VAD hysteresis and padded by 750 ms before and 300 ms after detected speech. ONNX Runtime's WebAssembly runtime is initialized with the Silero model before WebGPU setup, without a deadline; Granite WebGPU setup and warmup then have a 30-second deadline. A WebGPU error falls back to WASM in the same worker. A missed deadline restarts the transcription in a fresh WASM-only worker, and WebGPU stays disabled until the page is reloaded.

In cross-origin-isolated pages ONNX Runtime uses up to four WebAssembly threads: the transcription worker plus a pool of nested pthread workers. Emscripten ignores a pool worker that fails to load and would otherwise wait forever for it, so the transcription worker detects that failure and the transcription restarts in a fresh single-threaded worker. Later transcriptions stay single-threaded until the page is reloaded; WebGPU inference is unaffected.

Transcription logs timestamped `[Granite]` diagnostics to the browser console at the Info level: per-asset cache hits, downloads, and cache writes; ONNX Runtime WebAssembly compilation and pthread-worker startup; Granite session creation and warmup; and per-segment inference. Any stage running longer than 5 seconds logs a heartbeat every 5 seconds, listing unfinished cache writes.

Audio imports wait for browser-storage writer initialization before streaming decoded samples, so a first import in a fresh browser profile cannot complete as an empty clip.

Regeneration replaces every transcript word that intersects the selected clip-source interval, including a word that straddles a selection boundary, while preserving transcript ranges wholly outside it. Repeated placements of one clip share the same clip-owned transcript. Granite emits one sharp CTC spike per word, usually near its onset, so word timing combines that spike with speech activity (the speech mask plus energy relative to nearby speech). A word starts at its spike, or 64 ms before speech resumes when its spike sits in a pause; it ends where its speech stops, bridging quiet stretches of up to 96 ms (stop closures) or up to 192 ms while the word is still shorter than its letters suggest (quiet fricatives such as the /st/ of "states"). An end never passes the next word's start or a length cap of 90 ms per letter (at least 300 ms). Granite can also emit a segment's first word in the silence at the segment's start (the 750 ms VAD pre-roll or a 20-second chunk's leading pause); such a word starts at the nearest supported speech before the next word. Boundaries are estimates on 32 ms acoustic frames, not phoneme-accurate alignments.
